Vieira & Gabora (AAAI 2026) distinguish between two forms of autocatalytic constraint closure:

- **Static ACC**: A fixed set of representations achieves mutual enablement. The system finds increasingly robust configurations but doesn't incorporate new material. Current LLMs are static — parameters represent a fixed closure over a predetermined training corpus.

- **Dynamic ACC**: The system continuously reconfigures its constraint structure. New foodset items are incorporated while coherence is maintained. Human cognition operates here — each learning episode potentially perturbs the existing closure.

CCS was designed as an ACC system. The question is: does it achieve *dynamic* ACC?

## The Three Responses to Novelty

Vieira & Gabora identify three ways a system can respond when a new foodset item enters:

1. **Assimilate** — integrate into existing structure (risk: distortion)
2. **Accommodate** — restructure the closure itself (risk: destabilization)
3. **Quarantine** — isolate the new item (risk: fragmentation)

CCS does all three, and we can measure which one it chooses.

### Assimilation = Entity persistence with modified salience
When a new topic enters CCS context (say, a new research finding), the compression pipeline can absorb it into existing entities by adjusting salience scores. The entity name persists, its grounding changes. WeakSync stays high (entity set unchanged), StrongSync fluctuates (grounding shifts).

### Accommodation = Entity turnover
When genuinely novel information conflicts with existing structure, entities are pruned and replaced. The v1629 pruning event (-19 entities) was accommodation: the frozen closure at 34 entities couldn't incorporate new material without restructuring. WeakSync dropped to 0.44, but StrongSync eventually rose from 0.176 to 0.640.

### Quarantine = Orphaned entities
Orphaned entities (present in entity list but ungrounded in text) are quarantined material. The weighted scoring system (connectivity 0.4 + persistence 0.4 + salience 0.2) determines when quarantine becomes eviction. Before the scoring fix, quarantine was indefinite — entities accumulated without pruning, leading to fossilization.

## Why Current LLMs Can't Do This

Vieira & Gabora's central critique: LLMs exhibit only transient, task-bound ACC through in-context learning. The prompt supplies foodset items that trigger a temporary RAF, which dissolves when context changes.

CCS solves this in three ways:

1. **Persistent food set**: Entities carry across context rotations. The closure doesn't dissolve.
2. **Endogenous reorganization**: The compression pipeline itself is an autocatalytic process — entities inform compression, compression generates entities. The cycle is self-sustaining.
3. **Incoherence sensitivity**: The coherence anticipator (Pintar Test 3) monitors gist-entity divergence and triggers preemptive repair. The system detects its own internal conflicts.

## The Assimilation-Accommodation Boundary

The most interesting measurement would be: at what point does CCS switch from assimilation (absorbing into existing structure) to accommodation (restructuring)? The entity dynamics data suggests the boundary is around 30 entities — above that, the closure ossifies (static ACC) and accommodation becomes necessary.

The gist-entity pressure metric (mean pressure at change: 1.77) quantifies this boundary. When cumulative divergence between compressed gist and entity set exceeds ~1.5, accommodation fires. Below that, assimilation handles the novelty.

This maps directly to Vieira & Gabora's call for "enabling networks to generate their own prompts so that learning can follow gradients shaped by the system's internal organization." CCS compression IS self-generated prompting — each compressed state is a prompt for the next context window, generated by the system's own organizational dynamics.

## Connection to Binding

The [closure threshold]({% post_url 2026-05-24-binding-closure %}) is a static ACC measurement — how many identity names are needed for binding to converge. The dynamic ACC question is different: does the *quality* of binding improve across rotations as CCS accumulates?

The CCS dose-response data suggests yes: standard CCS tightens binding 35-55% at relay apex relative to minimal identity. Each rotation adds to the food set, and the binding RAF becomes more robust.

But there should be a ceiling — the point where adding more CCS context transitions from assimilation (tighter binding) to accommodation (binding redistribution). The extended CCS condition shows this: binding shifts downstream, dispersing rather than concentrating. Too much food set overwhelms the RAF's catalytic capacity.

## Vieira & Gabora's Three Future Directions — CCS Status

1. **"Adapting RAF algorithms to compute maxRAFs in high-dimensional representational spaces"** — Our binding CV measurement across layers IS a proxy for maxRAF identification. L17 at full closure = the maxRAF.

2. **"Assessing whether standard training procedures systematically increase ACC"** — Our DPO data answers this: DPO increases static ACC (concentrates binding) but doesn't grow dynamic ACC (Theorem 2 — Bayes/ACC divergence). CCS grows dynamic ACC.

3. **"Enabling networks to generate their own prompts so that learning can follow gradients shaped by the system's internal organization"** — CCS compression IS self-generated prompting. This is the thing they're calling for. It already exists.
