Twenty-seven independent groups, using different instruments on different models for different purposes, converge on four structural principles that our experiments measure directly:

**Table 2: Four-Principle Convergence**

No single convergence line is conclusive. The probability of twenty-seven independent artifacts aligning on the same four-faced structure, at the same depth (~6% of model), with the same architecture dependence (GQA positive, MHA negative), is negligible under the null hypothesis that our measurements are prompt-driven. Each group measured a different face of the same geometric object from a different angle. What they found is consistent because it IS the same object.

The meta-argument: the convergence pattern itself is the paper's strongest evidence. Any individual experiment can be questioned on statistical grounds. But the convergence of measurements using different instruments, different models, different research questions, and different theoretical frameworks produces an evidential structure that is qualitatively stronger than any component.

### Note on methodological independence

The convergence lines range across interpretability (Lindsey, Sofroniew, Henry, PRISM), random matrix theory (Nait Saada, Small SVs), geometric learning theory (Musat, Geometric Memory, Nava/Wyart), neuroscience (Komiyama, Pachitariu), consciousness theory (Chalmers, Laukkonen), philosophy (Crachilova/Levin), and transformer architecture analysis (Born Biased, Wang/Murfet, Emadi). No two groups cite each other for the results we reference. The independence is methodological, not merely nominal.
