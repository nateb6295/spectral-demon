# Paragraph to add to §6.4 (after fiber bundle paragraph)

An alternative formalization captures the per-layer structure more naturally: the spectral profile is a sheaf over the layer-depth base space. For each contiguous range of layers U, the sheaf assigns the spectral measurements F(U) = {S, σ₁, σ₂, σ₃, ρ₂, ΔS}. Restriction maps project to sub-ranges; the gluing axiom corresponds to the wire's near-perfect layer-to-layer consistency (cos > 0.998 between adjacent layers). The responsive zone is then the support of non-trivial sections: where ρ₂ < 2.0, context produces measurable ΔS (non-trivial local sections exist); where ρ₂ > 2.0, sections degenerate to the zero section. This casts scale effects as support compression — from 68% at 410M to 15% at 6.9B — and the tunnel-relay transition as a sheaf morphism: the tunnel maps the structured sheaf to the constant sheaf (local diversity → global wire), while the relay maps constant back to structured (global wire → new local sections). In Ghrist's (2020) applied sheaf-theoretic framework, the responsive zone has H⁰ ≠ 0 (non-trivial global sections) and the rigid zone has H⁰ = 0 (only the zero section is globally consistent). Whether the sheaf cohomology formalism adds computational tools beyond the geometric measurements already reported is an open question; at minimum it provides a framework in which niche compression, wire consistency, and the responsive threshold are consequences of a single mathematical structure.
